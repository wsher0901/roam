## The story

2026-08-07 19:50 UTC · TWO RULINGS, AND A CORPUS THAT HAD TO AGREE
· work PC

**THE CHOOSER IS NOT AN ADDITIVE RULE, AND PRETENDING IT WAS
WOULD HAVE BEEN THE EASY BENCH.** "≤4 teammates, each on its own
bench" and
[D-055](../record/DECISIONS.md#d-055--agent-teams-the-boundary)'s
"a team never authors a bench" are the same sentence with opposite
verdicts. Filing the chooser and leaving D-055 standing would have
left both halves reading as current — **a corpus that contradicts
itself is worse than one that is out of date**, because nothing
tells a reader which half is live.
[D-082](../record/DECISIONS.md#d-082--the-vehicle-chooser)
therefore names its amendments, and
[HOME §Agent teams](../HOME.md#agent-teams)' boundary sentence is
recut rather than annotated.

**WHAT DID NOT MOVE IS THE POINT.** D-055's two risks stand
verbatim: a team is SINGLE-SITTING-ONLY, and a TEAMMATE-AUTHORED
DIFF IS SELF-AUTHORED, taking external Web review before the word.
The recut is about AUTHORSHIP, not supervision — D-055's reasoning
was that teams share one working tree with no PR isolation, and
the chooser ANSWERS that reasoning by giving each teammate its own
bench under unrelaxed lane law, rather than waiving it. A rule
that survives its amendment intact is a rule that was about the
right thing.

**THE SWEEP WAS WALKED, AND IT SPLIT TWO WAYS.** Four chronicle
stories name D-055's boundary. The lazy reading treats all four as
defects; the enumeration law says walk the real inputs from their
source. Two carry it in their CURRENT ENDING —
[agent-teams](../chronicle/agent-teams.md) and
[lane-vehicles](../chronicle/lane-vehicles.md), the latter calling
a team "one NAMED EXCLUSION" — and those endings are now false.
Two carry it in a SPINE BULLET NARRATING D-055 —
[lane-law](../chronicle/lane-law.md) and
[team-aware-leaving](../chronicle/team-aware-leaving.md) — and
those are true as of their date and must NOT be edited: a spine
records what was decided when, and rewriting it would destroy the
history the ending rule exists to preserve. **The same sentence in
two positions is two different obligations.**

**THE BLOCKER RULE IS THIS WEEK'S OWN LESSON, WRITTEN DOWN.** On
2026-08-06 the arbiter went silent for about ninety minutes and my
reports said so — accurately, in paragraph four, under a heading
about what had been achieved. Everything stated was true and the
one fact governing what the founder could do was architecturally
invisible. Rule 9 fixes the wording so it cannot be softened by
whoever writes it, puts it ABOVE the verdict slot, and repeats it
every turn, because a blocker announced once and then dropped
reads as cleared.

**AND THE SECOND RULING'S BEST ARGUMENT IS THE FIRST RULING'S
FATE.** Both were given in the Web ops chat on 2026-08-06/07, and
**the chooser was lost to the outage** — spoken into a channel the
repo does not read, on a day when the session's attention was on a
stalled campaign. It survived only because the founder repeated it
here. A ruling that lives only in a chat is one distraction away
from never having happened; that is precisely what rule 9 is
guarding against, one layer up.
