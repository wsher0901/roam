## The story

**%s — the D-088 re-cut, before any vetting.**
[D-088](../record/DECISIONS.md#d-088--product-the-september-re-tailoring--retrieval-the-optimizer-cost-trend-state)
merged into this branch from main on claim, and two of its rulings
are this bench's duty rather than background. Ruling 3 says the
T3–T6 benches add their retrieval-policy rows WHEN THEY RESPAWN —
this is that respawn, so the duty was written into
[the spec](../record/specs/crowds-calendar.md) as its own section
plus two Done-means boxes, rather than carried in the lane's head.
Ruling 9 rewrote [CC-07](../data/FACTS.md#f-cc-07--trending-signal-computed): trend is a COMPUTED signal from
proxies, so the `trending-general` slot stopped being "find a trend
source" and became "vet four proxies, and find out which of them we
may legally keep the derived numbers from". That reframing is the
largest single change to this bench's work.

**2026-09-15 — the trend proxies, decided on their terms pages.**
[CC-07](../data/FACTS.md#f-cc-07--trending-signal-computed)'s four
proxies were each taken to the provider's own licence text rather
than to its marketing page, because
[D-015](../record/DECISIONS.md#d-015--data-asset-law) turns on
retention and nothing else.

- **REDDIT IS DROPPED FROM THE V1 PROXY SET.** Its Data API Terms
  §3.1 require a separate agreement for commercial purposes, §3.2
  forbids deriving revenue from the APIs without express written
  approval, and §6 says that on termination — which Reddit may
  trigger "for any reason and for any period of time, without
  notice" — we must delete cached content AND "any data or models
  that were derived from" it. A derived series that a counterparty
  can order deleted without notice is not an asset-layer asset.
- **YOUTUBE SURVIVES, BUT CACHE-ONLY WITH A 30-DAY CEILING.**
  Developer Policies III.E.4: "an API Client must not store
  statistics retrieved as Non-Authorized Data for more than 30
  days". A rolling velocity window fits inside that; a long-run
  retained time-series does not. So the proxy is licensable and the
  ASSET is not — which is a different answer from "yes" and had to
  be written down as one.
- **RESERVATION SCARCITY IS DROPPED.** No self-serve public
  reservation API was reachable; partner programmes exist, and a
  public product cannot assume admission to one.
- **REVIEW VELOCITY IS NOT THIS BENCH'S TO VET.** It differences
  snapshots of a venue source
  [V1.S1.T4](../ROADMAP.md#v1s1--data-definition-the-gate-docs--spike-scripts-only-no-app-code)
  owns; vetting it here would vet a sibling's slot.

A second gotcha, recorded because it nearly produced a false
finding: developers.google.com serves a LOCALISED policy page when
no `Accept-Language` header is sent. The first run grepped the
English clause against an Italian page and reported NOT FOUND — a
clean result from a bad probe, which the sweep law names as the
more dangerous kind. The spike now pins `?hl=en` plus the header.

**2026-09-15 — completion, and the critic round that changed the
deliverable.** Ten findings against my own diff. Nine were wording
fixes; the first was not.

1. **A GRADE RESTING ON A CHECK I NEVER RAN.** `public-holidays` was
   graded B "where both indexes agree" — a rule I described and did not
   execute. Running it found the first draft's test would also have been
   the WRONG one: comparing counts, Spain reads 32 against 54 and looks
   like disagreement, while the two agree on every national holiday
   because they index different scopes. Compared over NATIONWIDE DATES
   across ten countries, the statutory core is identical and every
   disagreement sits at two edges — Easter Sunday (a Sunday, so it
   shuts nothing extra) and de facto non-statutory days, of which
   Sweden alone has three. That last one inverts the reading: the
   disagreement is a REASON to keep both sources, not a defect, because
   Christmas Eve in Sweden is exactly the day a traveler needs warning
   about.
2. **Calendarific was rejected on a guess.** Its own pricing page claims
   230+ countries and 3,300+ regions — BROADER than either source I
   chose, and it would close the India/Pakistan/Thailand gap outright.
   Rewritten from "rejected" to PARKED, with the two real reasons it is
   out of V1: a key, and a free tier of 500 calls per year.
3. Socrata's anonymous-throttling claim was unsourced → now quoted from
   `dev.socrata.com/docs/app-tokens`.
4. PredictHQ "priced for enterprise" was unverified — its pricing page
   returns HTTP 405 to a plain GET → downgraded to not-verified-here.
5. "No published rate limit" on the holiday APIs was an absence asserted
   from not having seen one → "not stated on the pages read".
6. "Regional authorities publish 6–12 months ahead" was unmeasured →
   replaced with what the spike actually showed.
7. "Popular Times is a Maps product surface" was an inference written as
   a finding → evidence and inference separated.
8. UN Tourism "behind subscription" → a deferral, not a finding.
9. Google Trends "no official API" (twice) → "no official public API was
   found", not verified against a terms page.
10. Foursquare/SafeGraph "enterprise pricing and consent questions" →
    downgraded; neither was verified.
