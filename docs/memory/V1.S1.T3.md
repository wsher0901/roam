## The story

**2026-09-15 — the spec gains D-088 ruling 3's duty.** The ack
landed and the first act after it was NOT source work: origin/main
had moved under this bench, and
[D-088](../record/DECISIONS.md#d-088--product-the-september-re-tailoring--retrieval-the-optimizer-cost-trend-state)
ruling 3 names T3–T6 as adding their own retrieval-policy rows WHEN
THEY RESPAWN. So the merge came first, then the spec
([sky-sea](../record/specs/sky-sea.md)) gained a section fixing the
row's three fields, the three rules that bind it (`n/a` says why ·
the policy is per fact and an entry-level row declares itself a
shorthand · grade B is domain CLASS per
[ENGINE §3](../ENGINE.md#3-acquire--get-the-facts), never a
promotion by the row that lists the domain), a corrected Plan step 5
and a new Done-means checkbox. Plan step 5 had to be corrected
rather than extended: it said "LLM-research grade", a phrase
[D-088](../record/DECISIONS.md#d-088--product-the-september-re-tailoring--retrieval-the-optimizer-cost-trend-state)
ruling 1 RETIRED because it collapsed rung 5a and 5b and so hid the
better one.

**2026-09-15 — seven slots vetted, every grade backed by a live run.**
The bench's rule was that a grade with no run is an assertion, so
seven zero-dependency spikes were written and run, and each entry
records the command and what the real response returned.

The calls that were NOT obvious, and why they went the way they did:

- **astro-ephemeris is computed, not fetched, and the proof is a
  cross-check.** Rung 3 answers SS-01..03 exactly everywhere, so the
  spike implements the NOAA solar equations and a Meeus lunar model
  and compares them against the US Naval Observatory: worst sun-event
  delta 3 minutes, moon illumination exact to the percent at three
  sites. USNO is the ORACLE, never the source — and the computed path
  covers astronomical twilight, which USNO's `oneday` service does
  not return at all.
- **Two gaps were MEASURED rather than described.** "Tides are patchy
  outside the US" became: nearest NOAA station 4,847 km from
  Mont-Saint-Michel, 2,479 km from Sydney, 2,567 km from Jeju.
  "Nature timing is weak" became: 12 of 12 US Spring-Index queries
  returned a value, 0 of 8 non-US did, and a mechanical walk of all
  129 USA-NPN layer names found spring index and growing-degree-days
  present, **foliage and wildlife migration absent entirely**.
- **night-sky-darkness has no source at all, and the spike proves it
  four ways** — key wall, login wall, imagery-only, and a licence.
  The licence is the one worth keeping: the Falchi World Atlas 2015,
  the dataset a hurried vetting WOULD have picked, is **CC BY-NC**,
  quoted from its publisher's own page — a
  [D-015](../record/DECISIONS.md#d-015--data-asset-law) disqualifier.
  It is named in the entry precisely so nobody re-adopts it later on
  reputation.
- **A validation that failed the naive test was kept, not tuned
  away.** aurora-viability was checked against SWPC's OVATION
  nowcast; ranking the sites by geomagnetic latitude does NOT
  reproduce OVATION's probability ordering, because OVATION is
  magnetic-local-time dependent and Fairbanks was on the dayside. The
  honest check — nightside sites only — gives 46% inside the oval
  against 4% outside, and the limit is written into the entry:
  OVATION validates the gate's SHAPE and can never validate a
  season-scale verdict.
- **Two grades were SPLIT because one grade would have lied.**
  aurora-viability is B for the darkness gate (exact) and C for the
  Kp-threshold verdict (a curated rule of thumb). astro-events is A
  for eclipses (a published canon) and B for shower peaks (the IAU
  catalogue holds several published solutions per shower — the eta
  Aquariids span 12.6° of solar longitude, about twelve days, so the
  spread has to render).
- **Three retrieval policies are real rows; four slots write `n/a`
  with a reason.** The `n/a`s are not silence: astro-ephemeris,
  aurora-forecast, aurora-viability and astro-events each say WHY no
  rung-5a path exists, and SS-10 writes its own `n/a` separately from
  SS-04 because the two facts differ — so the tides row is split and
  names its fact IDs rather than shorthanding the entry.
- **Where a B tag needed justifying, it is justified in the entry.**
  DarkSky International is tagged B **only** for whether a place
  holds its own certification (it operates that programme), and C for
  any sky-brightness number it publishes. A university phenology
  group is tagged C: good science is not one of
  [ENGINE §3](../ENGINE.md#3-acquire--get-the-facts)'s domain
  classes, and grading it B would be the promotion the rule forbids.

**A dead end worth recording:** NOAA's geomag-web calculator API, the
obvious route to magnetic inclination per point, is now KEY-GATED —
it returned HTTP 400 with "Either the key parameter is missing or it
is wrong". The route taken instead is better anyway: fetch the IGRF-14
Gauss coefficients from IAGA/NOAA and derive the centred dipole pole
locally, which lands at 80.83°N 72.80°W — matching the published pole
position, and with no key, no quota and no typed constant.
