# The hands doctrine — solo, subagents, a team, or lanes

> **CURRENT ENDING (2026-08-06).** One litmus, filed verbatim at
> [SETUP §Models & effort](../SETUP.md#once-and-done--cloud-accounts):
> **many hands on ONE bench with the founder watching = a team ·
> many benches = lanes · reading only = subagents**, and solo is the
> default everything falls back to. The rule's job is to make the
> vehicle follow from the SHAPE OF THE WORK rather than the mood of
> the moment. Beside it, in the same section, sits the pick that
> governs the seat itself: **Opus 5 · xhigh, one pick for
> everything**, with a throttle order that reduces effort before
> downgrading the model. Two of the doctrine's original numbers have
> since been retired by verification rather than by opinion — the
> Sonnet-teammate default, and the critic's model — and both
> retirements are named forward from the entries that carried them,
> because the decision log is append-only.

## What it is

A choosing rule, stated as a litmus so it can be applied without
deliberation:

- **Solo** — sequential or single-file work. The default.
- **Exploratory subagents** — parallel READ-ONLY research inside
  one task. No spec needed, because nothing is authored.
- **An agent team** — ONE task splittable into file-disjoint
  subparts that benefit from cross-talk, with the founder present
  to steer. Never long-unattended, never ritual or law files, never
  across tasks.
- **Parallel lanes** — separate tasks on their own benches,
  unattended-capable.

The litmus reduces those four to three questions: how many benches,
who is watching, and is anything being written.

## What raised it

The absence of any rule at all. The same piece of work could be
handed to a team, a lane, or a solo pass with nothing saying which,
and the costly wrong pick — an unattended team turned loose on a
law file, say — had nothing to catch it. The founder stated the
rule verbatim alongside the Shakedown phase-2 kickoff, and it was
filed as doctrine the same way the pre-gate critic had been: written
by a lane, from the founder's own words.

## What changed, in order

- **[D-045](../record/DECISIONS.md#d-045--hands-doctrine) — the
  rule becomes doctrine, verbatim.** It is filed in SETUP rather
  than in the lane manual, because it is a founder-facing standing
  choice about how to spend hands, not a lane mechanic. The
  original text carried concrete numbers: lead plus 2–4 Sonnet 5
  teammates, start at 2; delegate-mode lead for pure coordination;
  plan-approval for teammates touching shared or hard-to-reverse
  surfaces.
- **[D-054](../record/DECISIONS.md#d-054--the-landing-skill) — the
  seat's model pick collapses to one.** Opus 5 · xhigh for
  everything, retiring the earlier Opus 4.8 / Sonnet 5 reliance
  pair; xhigh is the working ceiling, `max` and ultracode only on
  an explicit founder tag; Fable 5 is a second opinion and never
  the seat. The throttle order — effort down before model down —
  is the part that matters under usage limits, and it is stated so
  that a seat under pressure does not have to invent a policy.
- **[D-055](../record/DECISIONS.md#d-055--agent-teams-the-boundary)
  — two of the doctrine's numbers are retired BY VERIFICATION.**
  The Sonnet-teammate default goes, because teammates ride the
  lead's model through a `/config` setting and a teammate's model
  is fixed at spawn — so the only correct answer is a setting, not
  a number in a doctrine. And the critic's model is settled the
  same way: `/model` could not be run from the harness, so the
  `opus` alias was resolved MECHANICALLY from a headless run's own
  billing telemetry, which reported `canonicalModel:
  claude-opus-5`. The alias was then taken rather than a pin,
  because aliases survive deprecations.
- **The retirements were given forward pointers, which is the part
  worth copying.** The old entries stay unedited under the log's
  append-only law; the entry that retires them names both
  explicitly, and the live copy in SETUP carries the supersession
  inline. Without that, two decision entries would have stood
  contradicting a live file that claimed they had been superseded.

## Where it stands

The litmus is settled and does no arguing with anything: teams and
lanes each got a written place, subagents were never in dispute,
and solo remains what everything falls back to.

**The honest limit is that a judgement rule cannot be verified, and
SETUP says so in its own words: `VERIFY: none — a judgment rule,
not a setting.`** Every other line in that section can be checked
with a command; this one is checked only by whether the right
vehicle got picked, which nothing records. So the doctrine's
compliance is invisible by construction — a wrong pick leaves no
trace unless it causes a failure worth writing down.

One clause has been observed working, from the direction of what it
FORBIDS rather than what it permits. The rule says a team is for
one bench with the founder watching, and never for ritual or law
files. The workshop's first actual team use held that line exactly:
three read-only teammates researching a census, writing nothing to
the tree, at a ground seat, with the lead doing its own share. The
doctrine's most consequential sentence is the one that kept
authorship away from the vehicle that had no PR gate.
