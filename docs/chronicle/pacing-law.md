# the pacing law — attention is the scarce resource

> **CURRENT ENDING (2026-08-06).** A standing law in four clauses:
> the ongoing task and pending blocks outrank new tasks; at most
> "Now:" plus one parallel slot may need the founder's attention;
> fully-specified no-decision work may run as extra lanes at
> Claude's discretion; **stopping is a valid next step.** It is the
> workshop's only rule that protects a person rather than a file,
> and it survives intact in
> [LAWS](../LAWS.md#workflow-non-negotiable) with every ritual
> deferring to it. **Its one amendment has quietly lapsed.**
> [D-016](../record/DECISIONS.md#d-016--parallel-ready-menu) ruled
> that every briefing and completion ALWAYS appends a
> "Parallel-ready:" list of launchable tasks — and that list appears
> nowhere in the living system today. The rendered ritual shapes
> carry the CAP and not the MENU. Nothing recorded the reversal; the
> shapes were rewritten around it.

## What it is

The rule that governs how much the workshop is allowed to ask of the
founder at once. Its living text is one bullet in
[LAWS §Workflow](../LAWS.md#workflow-non-negotiable):

- the ongoing task and pending blocks **outrank** new tasks
  (finish-first ordering);
- at most **"Now:" plus one parallel slot** needs the founder's
  attention;
- fully-specified, no-decision `[P]` work **may run as extra lanes
  at Claude's discretion** — it costs no attention, so it is not
  capped;
- **"more" unlocks the full menu**, on request;
- **stopping is a valid next step.**

The distinction it draws is the whole point and is easy to misread:
**the cap governs ATTENTION, not throughput.** Lanes may widen
freely as long as widening them does not require the founder to
decide anything. What is rationed is the number of things asking to
be thought about.

## What raised it

A stated fact about the person the workshop serves, not an incident.
[D-009](../record/DECISIONS.md#d-009--pacing-law) records the
reasoning in one line: **a solo builder's scarce resource is
attention, not task supply.** Before it, briefings and completions
ended with full menus of everything that could be done next.

The failure mode named is specific — "endless next-lists cause
fatigue and half-finished lanes" — and the alternatives rejected
show what the law is balancing against. A RIGID cap was rejected for
wasting parallel capacity on work that needs no oversight; NO cap
was rejected for producing lane sprawl across two machines. The
flexible cap is the compromise, and it is flexible in exactly one
direction.

## What changed, in order

- **[D-009](../record/DECISIONS.md#d-009--pacing-law)
  ([#27](https://github.com/wsher0901/roam/pull/27)) — born, with
  "plate is full" as an honest answer.** Full menus are replaced by
  finish-first pacing. The clause most often forgotten is recorded
  here from the first day: **"plate is full" is itself an
  instruction, and stopping is a valid next step.**
- **[D-016](../record/DECISIONS.md#d-016--parallel-ready-menu)
  ([#39](https://github.com/wsher0901/roam/pull/39)) — the amendment
  that has since lapsed.** Every briefing and completion ALWAYS
  appends a "Parallel-ready:" list — every `[P]`, unblocked,
  unclaimed task, each labeled with its tool, its branch name, and a
  ready-to-paste worktree launch command. It is explicitly
  INFORMATIONAL and explicitly EXEMPT from the attention cap. The
  reasoning is a genuine tension worth preserving:
  [D-009](../record/DECISIONS.md#d-009--pacing-law)'s discretionary
  clause had made parallel capacity INVISIBLE unless asked for, and
  the founder wanted to see it. The menu's answer was to add
  visibility at zero attention cost — "more visibility, zero added
  attention cost", in the decision's own words.
- **[D-027](../record/DECISIONS.md#d-027--laws-polish)
  ([#89](https://github.com/wsher0901/roam/pull/89)) — the law loses
  its citation and keeps its why.** LAWS drops its four inline
  D-citations, the pacing bullet among them, and carries a one-line
  gloss instead; provenance moves to the consolidations. The rule is
  unchanged, its rationale re-homed.
- **[#134](https://github.com/wsher0901/roam/pull/134) — the law is
  consolidated into its final bullet.** The LAWS tightness pass
  (Option C) keeps each command and its one-line why while expelling
  procedure grain that belongs in a skill. The pacing law comes out
  the other side as the compact bullet standing today.
- **[D-052](../record/DECISIONS.md#d-052--response-doctrine) and the
  report shapes ([#250](https://github.com/wsher0901/roam/pull/250))
  — the rendered shapes are rewritten, and the menu does not
  reappear.** Six rituals had grown six layouts; they are replaced
  by fenced skeletons with one job each. Every skeleton carries a
  single "Next:" or "Your act" slot. **None carries a
  Parallel-ready list.** The decision does not repeal
  [D-016](../record/DECISIONS.md#d-016--parallel-ready-menu) and
  does not mention it; the shapes were simply drawn without it.

## Where it stands

**The law itself is in force, undisputed, and referenced rather than
copied** — [pickup](../skills/pickup.md),
[handoff](../skills/handoff.md) and [ship](../skills/ship.md) each
close by pointing at it, and [HOME](../HOME.md) carries its
one-line gloss in Terms. Nothing in the record argues against it and
nothing has amended its substance since 2026-06.

**Its amendment is dead in practice, and this story is where that is
first written down.** Derived at write time rather than taken from
the census: the string `Parallel-ready` appears nowhere in
`docs/skills/`, [LAWS](../LAWS.md), [HOME](../HOME.md),
[SETUP](../SETUP.md), [ROADMAP](../ROADMAP.md),
[ATLAS](../ATLAS.md) or [DASHBOARD](../DASHBOARD.md). It survives
only in [DECISIONS](../record/DECISIONS.md), in the frozen history
of the bench that shipped it, and in census spans. What the rituals
actually render is the CAP — "recommend exactly ONE next action plus
at most one parallel slot" — with no menu attached.

**The honest reading is lapse, not repeal.**
[D-016](../record/DECISIONS.md#d-016--parallel-ready-menu) was never
revisited; the report-shape rewrite drew six new templates and the
menu was not among the things carried across. So the two surfaces
disagree: the decisions log says every briefing ALWAYS appends the
list, and no briefing does. That is exactly the kind of drift the
retroactivity law exists to catch, and it went uncaught because a
convention change backfills the FILES it names — and a clause that
dies by omission names no file.

**Whether it should come back is a real question, not a repair to
perform quietly.** The tension
[D-016](../record/DECISIONS.md#d-016--parallel-ready-menu) named is
still live: without the menu, parallel capacity is once again
invisible unless the founder asks "more". Against that, the report
skeleton's governing principle is one job per surface, and a menu is
the second job. **This story states the contradiction and rules
nothing** — the ruling belongs to the founder via
[decide](../skills/decide.md), and a lane may not take it.

**Contradiction with the census cell, stated as the spec requires.**
The cell reads "At most Now + one parallel slot needs the founder;
[P] work at Claude's discretion; stopping is a valid next step" —
true, current, and silent on the fact that the second of its two
cited decisions no longer describes any surface. The cell lists
[D-016](../record/DECISIONS.md#d-016--parallel-ready-menu) in its
span and none of its content in its ending, which is precisely how
the lapse stayed invisible.
