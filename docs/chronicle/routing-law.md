# The routing law — one home per class

> **CURRENT ENDING (2026-08-06).** Every class of information has
> exactly ONE home. New information APPENDS there via that home's
> named vehicle; changed information UPSERTS in place via the same
> writer; there is never a second copy, because the weave links to a
> fact's home rather than duplicating it. LAWS binds the clause and
> [HOME §Where information goes](../HOME.md#where-information-goes)
> renders the table — a division settled at birth and never
> revisited. **The census cell's span is STALE**, ending at
> [#144](https://github.com/wsher0901/roam/pull/144) on 2026-07-16;
> the table has since grown rows for ATLAS, `chronicle/`, IDEAS
> §Closed and the box-master class. The cell's actual sentence —
> that this is the law most often cited when a later bench decides
> where something goes — has if anything been strengthened, and the
> sharpest evidence is that the law has twice been enforced against
> the constitution itself.

## What it is

A rule that answers one question — *where does this go?* — and
refuses to answer it twice.

It has two halves, and the second is the one that does the work.
ONE HOME per class is the memorable part. APPEND VS UPSERT is the
operative part: new information is appended by that home's named
vehicle, and changed information is upserted IN PLACE by the same
writer. Naming the writer is what makes the rule enforceable —
"status lives on the DASHBOARD" is a preference, while "status is
upserted by a ritual repaint and never hand-edited" is a rule a
session can obey or breach.

The table at HEAD routes roughly two dozen classes: task story to
its memory, shipped narrative to `record/history/`, chronology to
the ledger, permanent rules to LAWS, decisions to DECISIONS, plan
structure to ROADMAP, status to the DASHBOARD, diagrams to ATLAS,
settled-item stories to `chronicle/`, unvisited ideas to IDEAS
§Open and settled ones to §Closed, probe results to
`record/probes/`, inventory to SETUP, explanations to HOME,
identity to FOUNDATION.

## What raised it

The founder asked twice where appends belong.

That is the whole origin, and it is worth stating plainly because it
is the pattern the workshop keeps rediscovering: the router already
existed in LAWS, but no GLANCEABLE rendering of it did. A rule that
is technically present and practically unfindable gets asked about
twice, and the second asking is the signal.

## Where it stands

Standing, uncontested, and enforced — including upward.

The cell calls it "the law most often cited when a later bench
decides where something goes", and the derivation supports that with
a sharper claim: **this is the law that has twice been used to
overrule a decision the workshop had just made.** D-053 promoted a
command block into LAWS for a good reason; D-069 removed it for a
better one. The removal did not argue that the block was unhelpful —
it argued that the second copy had started to drift, which is the
precise harm one-home exists to prevent, and which nobody would have
noticed had the copy not been made.

The uncomfortable corollary is worth keeping visible. A law that
forbids duplication is easy to state and hard to notice breaking:
the second copy is always created for a defensible local reason, and
it is correct on the day it is written. Drift is the only symptom,
and drift is silent. Nothing in the workshop currently DETECTS a
duplicated class — the two catches on record were both made by a
human reading, not by a check.
