# The cockpit — the control tower online

> **CURRENT ENDING (2026-08-06).** The baton-holding cloud command
> seat: FULL authorship under the laws — it authors benches, births
> and acks lanes, reviews lane PRs as a non-author, merges on the
> founder's word, seats itself on the board, reports as a fleet
> table, and ends through [land](land.md). The census cell says
> exactly that and remains true. **What the cell could not yet
> say is that the seat has since FLOWN** — four flights by
> 2026-08-04, enough for
> [D-074](../record/DECISIONS.md#d-074--phase-1-closes-on-flights-3-and-4)
> to rule that phase 1 does NOT close on the shakedown alone: two
> designed-but-never-exercised paths remain, the desk takeover of a
> live cockpit and the failure drill. The seat works; the parts of
> it that only fire when something goes wrong are still, by their
> nature, the parts least exercised.

## What it is

One cloud session with full authorship, alive for one bounded
flight, holding the baton while it lives. Its standing job, in the
[charter](../COCKPIT-CHARTER.md)'s own words: ADVISE the founder on
fleet status and on each gate as it reaches decision, and EXECUTE
the founder's rulings from the phone. **It never merges on its own
advice.**

Everything else follows from that one sentence. It can author,
because a seat that can only read re-creates the problem it was
built to solve. It cannot self-approve, because
no-solo-approval is law. It reports fleet-table-first, because the
founder is reading on a phone and needs the state before the
argument. And it ends through a ritual rather than by stopping,
because a session that vanishes without landing abandons whatever
it was flying.

## What raised it

**The attestation haze.** After the Shakedown Flight the founder
could not reconstruct where-did-what across Friday's three
surfaces — the clerk, Remote Control, and the GitHub app. Each held
a SLIVER of command and NONE held authorship, so every action away
from the desk was split across surfaces that could not act and could
not explain themselves afterwards.

The diagnosis in
[D-046](../record/DECISIONS.md#d-046--flight-cockpit-the-control-tower-online)
is that three narrow surfaces are not a third of a command seat —
they are a haze. One surface with full authorship removes it:
everything the founder does away from the desk happens in one
session that can actually act, reports decision-shaped, and leaves
the ordinary paper trail.

## What changed, in order

- **[D-046](../record/DECISIONS.md#d-046--flight-cockpit-the-control-tower-online)
  — the seat is born, and takes a law with it.** Full authorship;
  liftoff auto-fires it; the clerk's retirement staged; Remote
  Control demoted to backstop. Riding in the same entry, and
  outliving the flight architecture entirely: **NO-SOLO-APPROVAL
  becomes law.** It had been practice since the first external
  review, and [#175](https://github.com/wsher0901/roam/pull/175)
  proved it load-bearing — the external review caught the
  resurrection incident a self-check had missed. **A rule that
  catches what its author cannot see must be law, not habit.** The
  entry also performs the VOCABULARY RENAME that makes older texts
  read strangely: cockpit now means the CLOUD command session, and
  control tower the GROUND one. Renamed in living doctrine only —
  history and prior decisions stay as written.
- **A read-only cockpit was considered and rejected.** It would have
  been a clerk v2, and **authorship is exactly what the haze
  evidence indicts**: answering without acting keeps three surfaces
  alive.
- **[D-051](../record/DECISIONS.md#d-051--self-seat-birth) — it
  seats itself.** Because the fire went blind, no one else knows the
  new session's URL. The cockpit derives its own from the session
  environment and repaints the board as its first act. A foreign
  live cockpit seat-stamp instead means it is the STRAY: push
  nothing, write nothing, name the live seat, stop.
- **[D-052](../record/DECISIONS.md#d-052--response-doctrine) and
  [D-067](../record/DECISIONS.md#d-067--the-report-skeleton-and-the-board-diet)
  — how it speaks.** Turn-end reports are decision-shaped and each
  one doubles as the phone push. The fleet table comes first — one
  row per lane, id · purpose · glyph · waiting-on — then what needs
  the founder's word, with the exact word and where to type it.
  **Every 🔴 names its fix AND where it happens.**
- **[D-048](../record/DECISIONS.md#d-048--cockpit-resilience) and
  [D-049](../record/DECISIONS.md#d-049--gh-second-path) — it learns
  to survive its own API.** Told separately in
  [the connector-resilience story](cockpit-connector-resilience.md).
- **[D-054](../record/DECISIONS.md#d-054--the-landing-skill) →
  [D-061](../record/DECISIONS.md#d-061--the-landing-doctrine-recut-to-three-scenarios)
  — it gains an ending.** [Land](land.md) becomes the ritual, and
  AUTO-LAND becomes a standing duty the seat owes ITSELF: in the one
  turn where no lanes are working and nothing awaits the founder, it
  runs the ritual unprompted. Nothing else prompts it — that is why
  the duty had to be written.
- **[D-062](../record/DECISIONS.md#d-062--the-wake-rule-generalized)
  — its tombstone rule stops being its own.** The cockpit's
  supersession clause generalizes to EVERY seat. What began as a
  quirk of a bounded cloud session became the workshop's rule for
  any seat that wakes to find the board no longer names it.
- **2026-08-04 — it flew, and the flying changed the plan.**
  [D-074](../record/DECISIONS.md#d-074--phase-1-closes-on-flights-3-and-4)
  records the measurement that forced the change: a cloud lane
  carrying doc-sized cargo runs about FIVE MINUTES from licence to
  completion, and the founder's word arrives well after. So every
  fence landed on a finished bench and the mid-work freeze property
  went unobserved three times. **The failure is structural, not bad
  luck** — a duty timed against "the fleet is mid-work" is
  unrunnable by construction on cargo this size. The answer is a
  drill that HOLDS AT A CHECKPOINT UNTIL ACKED, so the drill decides
  when mid-work ends rather than the cargo deciding for it.

## Where it stands

**Live, flown, and the standard away seat.** The
[charter](../COCKPIT-CHARTER.md) is the master text and it is a BOX
MASTER — the routine's saved prompt is a copy, and an edit that
never reaches the box means a routine-fired cockpit is born without
it, which fails SILENTLY. That is why the re-save rides the board's
Needs-you rather than anyone's memory.

**The charter's most careful passage is its first act**, and it
repays reading as a piece of design. The seat derives state from a
fresh clone, then matches the board's baton line against three
cases — self-seat, hold-and-report, stray birth — with two rules
wrapped around them. **Match on what the line SAYS, never on what it
omits.** And if no case matches cleanly, treat it as hold-and-ask,
because **holding costs a message; seating over a live commander
costs the baton.** A birth prompt claiming the session supersedes
someone is explicitly named as exactly the kind of contradiction the
board overrules — only the board grants supersession, never the
machinery that fired the session.

**What is still untested is what the flights have not yet needed.**
[D-074](../record/DECISIONS.md#d-074--phase-1-closes-on-flights-3-and-4)
names the two: the desk takeover of a LIVE cockpit holding a
decision, and the failure drill running the connector rungs, summon,
and the freeze checkpoint together as deliberate failures rather
than hoped-for ones. Both are designed, load-bearing, and never
exercised — the same class of risk that opened phase 1 in the first
place, which is the argument for not closing the phase without
them.
